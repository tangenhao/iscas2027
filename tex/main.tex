\begin{abstract}
Video Diffusion Transformers (VDiTs) incur high inference cost from iterative denoising. Caching and low-bit quantization reduce the number and cost of recomputed steps, respectively, but their direct composition exposes two coupled inefficiencies: full-precision cache indicators can be misaligned with quantized execution, while asymmetric spatial/temporal attention shapes fragment fixed-width processing arrays. We present \textbf{Q-DARE}, an algorithm--hardware co-design combining \emph{quantization-aligned caching} (QAC) with a \emph{dimension-adaptive reconfigurable engine} (DARE). QAC evaluates and calibrates cache relevance in the quantized domain, aligning cache decisions with the computation that is actually executed. DARE adapts output parallelism for $QK^{\mathsf T}$ and reduction parallelism for $PV$ on a shared datapath, mitigating shape fragmentation without duplicating the arithmetic array. On Open-Sora v1.2, Q-DARE achieves a VBench score of 76.61 versus 76.77 for the 16-bit baseline. The cycle-level evaluation achieves \QDAREGeoA$\times$ geomean end-to-end speedup and \QDAREEnergyGeoA$\times$ step-throughput-per-watt improvement over the measured A100 baseline.
\end{abstract}
