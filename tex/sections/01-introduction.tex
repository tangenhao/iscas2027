\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\IEEEPARstart{V}{ideo} Diffusion Transformers (VDiTs) enable high-quality video generation but repeatedly execute expensive Spatial-Temporal Diffusion Transformer (STDiT) blocks throughout denoising~\cite{zheng2024opensora}. For example, Open-Sora v1.2 executes 28 spatial--temporal block pairs over 30 sampling steps, making both redundant timestep computation and low-precision arithmetic attractive optimization targets. Timestep caching skips redundant STDiT evaluations across adjacent steps~\cite{liu2025teacache}, while post-training quantization lowers the cost of each recomputed step~\cite{zhao2025viditq}. Recent joint approaches further optimize cache schedules, error correction, or precision selection~\cite{liu2025cachequant,wu2025quantcache}. However, these techniques largely optimize \emph{when} to recompute and \emph{how precisely} to recompute as separate decisions. Their direct composition with a fixed W8A8/INT8 accelerator leaves two cross-layer mismatches unresolved.

The first is a \textbf{decision-domain mismatch}. Conventional cache policies estimate recomputation benefit from full-precision feature variation, whereas the recomputed path evolves according to quantized representations. Because quantization is nonlinear and piecewise constant, a large FP-domain change may be suppressed after quantization, while a seemingly small change may cross a quantization boundary. Consequently, an FP-domain cache indicator can be poorly aligned with the computation that is actually executed, reducing the reliability of cache decisions in a fixed W8A8/INT8 pipeline.

The second is a \textbf{matrix-axis mismatch}. Factorized spatial and temporal attention alternate highly asymmetric sequence lengths, and the varying token dimension appears on different GEMM axes in $QK^{\mathsf T}$ and $PV$. In our hardware characterization, temporal attention uses $T=19$ throughout, exposing the short-sequence regime on both GEMM axes. Thus, the same fixed-width array must accommodate a short output dimension in $QK^{\mathsf T}$ and a short reduction dimension in $PV$, causing lane fragmentation and padding waste.

Fig.~\ref{fig:overview} summarizes \textbf{Q-DARE}, our cross-layer algorithm--hardware co-design for these two mismatches. At the algorithm level, \textbf{quantization-aligned caching (QAC)} evaluates cache relevance in the same numerical domain as recomputation and calibrates the indicator against quantized output variation. At the hardware level, the \textbf{dimension-adaptive reconfigurable engine (DARE)} adapts output parallelism for $QK^{\mathsf T}$ and reduction parallelism for $PV$ on a shared datapath. Output-lane partitioning improves short-sequence occupancy, boundary-aware segmented reduction packs variable-length dot products without cross-segment interference, and replay-based address remapping avoids explicit K/V transposition.

Our contributions are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We identify the \textbf{decision-domain mismatch} in cache-quantized VDiT inference and propose QAC to align cache decisions with the quantized execution trajectory.
  \item We characterize the \textbf{matrix-axis mismatch} of factorized attention and design DARE to adapt both output and reduction parallelism on a shared datapath.
  \item We implement the co-design in RTL and evaluate it on Open-Sora v1.2, demonstrating competitive generation quality together with end-to-end performance and energy-efficiency gains.
\end{itemize}
