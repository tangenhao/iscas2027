\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{subsec:setup}

\subsubsection{Algorithm Setup}
We evaluate Open-Sora v1.2 STDiT3-XL/2~\cite{zheng2024opensora} on
VBench~\cite{huang2024vbench}. All methods generate 64-frame,
$512\times512$ videos with 30 rectified-flow steps under identical
prompts and sampling settings. We compare Original (FP16),
ViDiT-Q~\cite{zhao2025viditq} (linear-only W8A8),
TeaCache~\cite{liu2025teacache} (FP16 timestep caching), and Ours
(QAC with W8A8 quantization for both linear and attention operators).
We report VBench together with representative generated frames for
quantitative and qualitative quality evaluation.

\subsubsection{Hardware Setup}
We compare against ViDA~\cite{ding2025vida} as the custom-accelerator
baseline and NVIDIA A100~\cite{nvidiaA100} as the GPU baseline.
A cycle-level simulator models module execution and dependencies at
1\,GHz. For the A100 comparison, we scale to 241 cores, giving a nominal
624.672 INT8 TOPS, and assume ideal inter-core balance. For PPA, one
28-nm core is normalized to 7\,nm using the factors in
DeepScaleTool~\cite{sarangi2021deepscaletool}; the corresponding
241-core area and power are linearly scaled from the normalized core.
The A100 is measured directly on the same three Open-Sora workloads.
ViDA uses 1\,GHz and 256\,GB/s, but its paper does not report power.

\subsection{Hardware Evaluation}
\label{subsec:hw_eval}

\subsubsection{Comparison with ViDA}
Table~\ref{tab:vida_comparison} compares Q-DARE with
ViDA~\cite{ding2025vida} after technology normalization to 7\,nm.
The requested per-core scaling maps the 28-nm values of
4.2624\,mm$^2$ and 116.372\,mW to 0.134\,mm$^2$ and 43.6\,mW.
Linear replication to the 241-core equal-peak configuration gives
\QDARESystemAreaSeven\,mm$^2$ and \QDARESystemPowerSeven\,W.

ViDA prints only its 16.44$\times$ geomean A100 speedup. Digitizing its
three Open-Sora GPU bars gives 15.72/16.29/17.20$\times$ and reproduces
the printed geomean across all eight bars. We bridge these values through
our measured A100 latency in Table~\ref{tab:latency_comparison}; the
ViDA latency columns are thus estimates, not same-stack measurements.

\subsubsection{Comparison with GPU}
Table~\ref{tab:latency_comparison} compares the measured A100 with our
equal-peak projections. Ideal fusion reaches \QDAREFusedGeoA$\times$
geomean speedup; the zero-DMA, perfect-balance, full-overlap result is
an upper bound rather than measured RTL latency.

The 10.508-W value linearly scales 43.6\,mW/core and excludes NoC,
clock-tree, memory, I/O, and DRAM overhead. The source power netlist also
omits the PEA datapath, while A100 power covers a slightly wider window.
Thus, the \QDAREFusedGeoEnergyA$\times$ fused and
\QDAREUpperGeoEnergyA$\times$ upper-bound geomeans are optimistic
energy proxies, not silicon claims.
