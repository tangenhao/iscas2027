\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{subsec:setup}

\textbf{Model and quality.}
We evaluate the official Open-Sora v1.2.0 STDiT3-XL/2 configuration (hidden size 1152, 16 heads, head dimension 72, and 28 spatial plus 28 temporal blocks). Quality experiments follow the released 240p, 9:16, 51-frame recipe with 30 rectified-flow steps and CFG scale 7. We report mean VBench over the official prompt protocol.

\textbf{Quantization and caching.}
All quantized runs use fixed W8A8 execution for GEMM/attention arithmetic while retaining FP16 for VCU operations required by normalization and nonlinear functions. Quantizer scales are calibrated offline and frozen. QAC uses the same quantized representation as the recompute path; its calibration split and cache threshold are fixed before test evaluation. Cache ratio denotes the fraction of STDiT block evaluations reused over the 30-step schedule.

\textbf{Hardware.}
The accelerator contains one $36\times36$ INT8 PEA (1296 INT8 MACs), a 36-MAC FP16 VCU, 238.5\,KB on-chip SRAM, and eight 256-bit DMA channels. We synthesize it with Synopsys Design Compiler T-2022.03-SP2 using the TSMC 28-nm HPC+ 0.9-V typical view at 1\,GHz. The cycle-level simulator models PEA/VCU/DMA execution, dependencies, and fusion-group overlap. For consistency, the temporal $QK^{\mathsf T}$ and segmented-$PV$ microarchitecture results both use $T=19$. Technology-normalized 7-nm values use area, delay, and power scaling factors of 31.818, 1.264, and 2.667, respectively.

\subsection{Hardware Efficiency}
\label{subsec:hw_eval}

Table~\ref{tab:hw_comparison} summarizes the DC synthesis results and the technology-normalized comparison with ViDA~\cite{ding2025vida}. At 28\,nm, DC reports an area of 4.2624\,mm$^2$ and a power of 116.372\,mW at 1\,GHz. Technology normalization gives 0.134\,mm$^2$, 43.6\,mW, and an estimated $F_{\max}$ of 1.264\,GHz. For cross-accelerator performance comparison, we use 1\,GHz and 256\,GB/s, matching ViDA's evaluation setting rather than exploiting the higher normalized $F_{\max}$.
