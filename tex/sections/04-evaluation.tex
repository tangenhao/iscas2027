\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{subsec:setup}

\subsubsection{Algorithm Setup}
We evaluate Open-Sora v1.2 STDiT3-XL/2~\cite{zheng2024opensora} on
VBench~\cite{huang2024vbench}. All methods generate 64-frame,
$512\times512$ videos with 30 rectified-flow steps under identical
prompts and sampling settings. We compare Original (FP16),
ViDiT-Q~\cite{zhao2025viditq} (linear-only W8A8),
TeaCache~\cite{liu2025teacache} (FP16 timestep caching), and Ours
(QAC with W8A8 quantization for both linear and attention operators).
We report VBench together with representative generated frames for
quantitative and qualitative quality evaluation.

\subsubsection{Hardware Setup}
We compare against ViDA~\cite{ding2025vida} as the custom-accelerator
baseline and NVIDIA A100~\cite{nvidiaA100} as the GPU baseline.
The complete Q-DARE design, including logic and on-chip SRAM, is
synthesized in TSMC 28\,nm at 1\,GHz. Following
ViDA~\cite{ding2025vida}, its PPA is normalized to 7\,nm using
DeepScaleTool~\cite{sarangi2021deepscaletool}. A cycle-level simulator
models module execution and dependencies to obtain accelerator latency.
For the A100 comparison, Q-DARE is projected to 241 cores, giving a
nominal 624.672 INT8 TOPS, while the A100 is measured directly on the
same 64-, 256-, and 512-frame Open-Sora workloads. The ViDA comparison
follows its 1\,GHz frequency and 256\,GB/s off-chip bandwidth setting.

\subsection{Algorithm Quality}
\label{subsec:quality}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig/gen_img.pdf}
  \caption{Algorithm-level quality comparison on Open-Sora v1.2.
  VBench scores and representative frames are reported under the same
  prompts and sampling settings.}
  \label{fig:quality}
\end{figure}

Fig.~\ref{fig:quality} compares the original FP16 model with
ViDiT-Q, TeaCache, and our QAC-based inference. Ours achieves a VBench
score of 76.61, close to the original model (76.77), while combining
timestep caching with W8A8 execution for both linear and attention
operators. The representative frames show that the generated visual
content is well preserved under the proposed cache--quantization
co-design.

\label{subsec:hw_eval}

Q-DARE achieves 0.292--0.324$\times$ the video throughput of ViDA,
with a 0.31$\times$ geomean, while improving geomean area efficiency
by 7.10$\times$. This tradeoff reflects Q-DARE's substantially smaller
normalized area while retaining competitive end-to-end throughput.

\subsubsection{Comparison with GPU}
Fig.~\ref{fig:a100_comparison} compares absolute STDiT throughput and
energy efficiency with the measured A100 baseline. Across 64-, 256-,
and 512-frame workloads, Q-DARE achieves 2.90$\times$, 3.30$\times$,
and 3.55$\times$ higher STDiT throughput, respectively, corresponding
to a 3.24$\times$ geomean. The corresponding energy-efficiency gains
are 105.8$\times$, 113.9$\times$, and 113.2$\times$, with a
110.9$\times$ geomean.

\begin{figure}[t]

The Q-DARE points use the FSDB-calibrated schedule with the
regular-attention VCU reduced to the measured PEA service floor.
They are projected architecture results rather than complete full-block
RTL measurements, whereas the A100 results are directly measured.
