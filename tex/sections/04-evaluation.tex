\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{subsec:setup}

\textbf{Model and quality evaluation.}
We use the official Open-Sora v1.2.0 release configuration and its
STDiT3-XL/2 backbone (hidden size 1,152, 16 heads, head dimension 72,
MLP ratio 4, and 28 spatial plus 28 temporal blocks). For the quality
experiments, we follow the released 240p, 9:16, 51-frame inference recipe
with 30 rectified-flow sampling steps and classifier-free guidance (CFG)
scale 7. The VAE uses an $8\times8$ spatial and $4\times$ temporal
compression with a 17-frame micro-frame window; consequently, the official
51-frame input is processed as three 17-frame chunks and produces a temporal
latent length of $T=15$. We report the mean VBench score over the official
prompt protocol; prompt list, checkpoint hash, and generated-video archive
are recorded with each experiment artifact.

\textbf{Quantization and caching protocol.}
All quantized runs use fixed W8A8 execution (INT8 activations and weights)
for GEMM/attention arithmetic, with FP16 retained for the VCU operations
required by normalization and nonlinear functions. Quantizer scales are
calibrated offline and then frozen for inference. QAC calibration uses the
same quantized representation and recompute path as evaluation; the cache
threshold is selected on a calibration split and kept fixed for the test
split. Unless a table explicitly states otherwise, the reported cache ratio
is the fraction of STDiT block evaluations reused over the 30-step schedule,
and quality is measured with identical prompts and decoding settings across
all methods. The software evaluation uses deterministic execution and logs
the random seed and calibration manifest alongside each score. The RTL
temporal-attention regression uses the repository default seed 20260831; the
cycle-model command line, synthesis reports, and generated manifests are
retained under the corresponding experiment output directory. Because this
repository does not currently pin a Python/CUDA lockfile, the final artifact
should additionally record the Python, PyTorch, CUDA, GPU, and checkpoint
versions used for each VBench run.

\textbf{Hardware workload and baselines.}
For cycle-level hardware evaluation, we additionally use the repository's
reproducible \texttt{stdit-512-64f} preset: 512$\times$512 resolution,
64 input frames, 17-frame VAE chunks, latent $T=19$, spatial token grid
$32\times32$ ($S=1,024$), effective CFG batch 2, and 19 executed STDiT
forwards after quantization/cache. This preset measures the 28 spatial and
28 temporal block stacks only; T5, VAE, embedding/final projection,
scheduler updates, PCIe setup, and instruction-download time are excluded
from the block-only latency number. We compare (i) a fixed-width padded
36-lane PEA, (ii) valid-count and segmented-reduction scheduling without
packing, and (iii) the proposed dimension-elastic schedule with four-head
$QK^{\mathsf T}$ packing, segmented $PV$ reduction, K/V replay, and
transpose-free address generation. Functional RTL checks cover temporal
lengths $T\in\{13,19,26,51\}$ and odd/even reduction tails; the default
$T=15$ path is reported as a fallback until the short-$T$ segmented mode is
verified.

\textbf{RTL, synthesis, and performance model.}
The RTL is synthesized with Synopsys Design Compiler T-2022.03-SP2 using the
TSMC 28-nm HPC+ typical 0.9-V, 25$^\circ$C standard-cell view and the
compiler NLDM views for the on-chip SRAM macros. We constrain all active
clocks to a 1.0-ns period (1 GHz), with 10\% clock uncertainty, source
latency, and network latency; data input/output delays are 10\% of the
clock period and the output load is 0.2 pF, as specified in
\texttt{dc/pea\_tsmc28/constraints/npu\_top.constraints.tcl}. The design
contains one physical compute cluster with a $36\times36$ INT8 PEA
(1,296 INT8 MACs), a 36-MAC FP16 VCU datapath, and 238.5 KB on-chip SRAM.
The four load routers expose eight independent 256-bit DMA channels,
corresponding to 32 B/cycle per channel and 256 GB/s aggregate bandwidth at
1 GHz. Power is taken from the synthesis/power flow; when activity files are
used, the RTL VCD/FSDB is generated by the documented temporal-attention
regression and mapped before power analysis.

Full-workload latency is obtained from the cycle-level simulator in
\texttt{sim/cycle\_model/stdit\_cycle\_sim.py}. It models PEA, VCU, DMA,
operator dependencies, and fusion-group overlap, and reports cycles at the
evaluated clock frequency. Area, power, and timing are taken from DC rather
than inferred from the cycle model. The 7-nm values in Table~\ref{tab:hw_comparison}
are technology-normalized estimates (area, delay, and power factors
31.818, 1.264, and 2.667) and are clearly distinguished from measured 28-nm
results.

\subsection{Implementation and Technology Normalization}
\label{subsec:hw_eval}

Table~\ref{tab:hw_comparison} summarizes the DC synthesis results and the
technology-normalized comparison with ViDA~\cite{ding2025vida}. At 28\,nm,
DC reports an area of \AreaTwentyEight{} and a power of
\PowerTwentyEight{} at 1\,GHz. Following the scaling methodology adopted by
ViDA, we normalize the 28-nm synthesis results to 7\,nm using area, delay,
and power scaling factors of \AreaScaleFactor{}, \DelayScaleFactor{}, and
\PowerScaleFactor{}, respectively.
This gives a normalized area of \AreaSevenNorm{}, power of
\PowerSevenNorm{}, and an estimated $F_{\max}$ of \FmaxSevenNorm{}.

For cross-accelerator performance comparison, we nevertheless use 1\,GHz and
256\,GB/s, matching ViDA's evaluation setting rather than exploiting the
higher normalized $F_{\max}$. ViDA reports 640 FP16 MACs and 2.03\,mm$^2$ at
7\,nm, but does not report accelerator power. Because the two designs use
different MAC counts and precisions, Table~\ref{tab:hw_comparison} is used to
state the implementation context; speedup claims are reported separately
under explicitly matched frequency and bandwidth assumptions.
