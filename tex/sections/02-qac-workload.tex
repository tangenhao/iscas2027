\section{Quantization-Aligned Caching}
\label{sec:qac}

\subsection{Decision-Domain Mismatch}

Let $F_t$ denote the low-cost feature used by a timestep-cache policy before
the expensive STDiT computation at denoising step $t$. A conventional cache
indicator measures the normalized variation of this feature in the
full-precision (FP) domain:
\begin{equation}
 d_t^{\mathrm{FP}}=
 \frac{\lVert F_t-F_{t-1}\rVert_1}
      {\lVert F_{t-1}\rVert_1+\epsilon}.
 \label{eq:fp_indicator}
\end{equation}
The cache predictor then maps $d_t^{\mathrm{FP}}$ to the expected effect of
skipping the current STDiT evaluation. This assumption is appropriate when
both calibration and recomputation follow the same FP trajectory, but it is
not guaranteed after the recompute path is replaced by fixed W8A8/INT8
execution.

To make this distinction explicit, let $Y_t^{\mathrm{Q}}$ denote the output
obtained by recomputing step $t$ with the quantized STDiT, and let
\begin{equation}
 r_t^{\mathrm{Q}} = \Delta\!\left(Y_t^{\mathrm{Q}},Y_{t-1}^{\mathrm{Q}}\right)
 \label{eq:q_output_variation}
\end{equation}
denote the output-variation target used by cache calibration. Here
$\Delta(\cdot,\cdot)$ denotes the same output-change metric used by the QAC
calibration procedure. Because quantization contains rounding, clipping, and
piecewise-constant regions, the ordering induced by $d_t^{\mathrm{FP}}$ need
not be preserved in $r_t^{\mathrm{Q}}$: an FP change may be suppressed by
quantization, while a small FP perturbation may cross a quantization boundary.
Consequently, a predictor calibrated in the FP domain can mis-rank the
recomputation importance of adjacent denoising steps. We refer to this
misalignment as the \emph{decision-domain mismatch}.

\subsection{Quantization-Aligned Indicator}

QAC removes this mismatch by evaluating cache relevance in the same numerical
domain as the recompute path. Using the calibrated quantizer scale $s$, we
first form
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde F_t = D\!\left(Q_s(F_t)\right),
 \label{eq:q_feature}
\end{equation}
where $Q_s(\cdot)$ and $D(\cdot)$ denote quantization and dequantization,
respectively. The quantization-aligned indicator is then
\begin{equation}
 d_t^{\mathrm{Q}}=
 \frac{\lVert \widetilde F_t-\widetilde F_{t-1}\rVert_1}
      {\lVert \widetilde F_{t-1}\rVert_1+\epsilon}.
 \label{eq:q_indicator}
\end{equation}
Unlike $d_t^{\mathrm{FP}}$, $d_t^{\mathrm{Q}}$ is computed from the numerical
representation seen by the low-precision model. QAC reuses the same calibrated
quantizer as the W8A8/INT8 recompute path rather than introducing a separate
precision mode or an auxiliary correction network. Thus, quantization affects
both the executed arithmetic and the signal used to decide whether that
arithmetic should be executed.

This design choice is also important for the hardware co-design. QAC changes
\emph{when} a denoising step is recomputed, but does not change the precision
of a recomputed step: all executed GEMM and attention operations remain on the
same fixed W8A8/INT8 datapath. The accelerator therefore does not need to
support step-dependent mixed-precision switching to exploit timestep reuse.

\subsection{Calibration and Runtime Cache Policy}

During offline calibration, QAC collects the quantization-aligned indicator
and the corresponding quantized-model output variation, forming samples of
$(d_t^{\mathrm{Q}},t,r_t^{\mathrm{Q}})$. A lightweight mapping
\begin{equation}
 \widehat r_t^{\mathrm{Q}} = g_q\!\left(d_t^{\mathrm{Q}},t\right)
 \label{eq:qac_mapping}
\end{equation}
is fitted to predict the recomputation significance along the quantized
trajectory. Including the denoising step $t$ allows the mapping to account for
the fact that the same local feature variation may have different effects at
different stages of the diffusion trajectory.

At runtime, the low-cost feature $F_t$ is available before the expensive STDiT
computation. QAC quantizes this feature with the frozen calibration scale,
computes $d_t^{\mathrm{Q}}$, and evaluates $g_q$. The resulting predicted
variation is consumed by the same thresholded reuse policy as the baseline
timestep cache: the step is reused while the accumulated predicted variation
remains within the cache budget, and a recomputation is triggered when the
policy requires the cached state to be refreshed. Therefore, QAC modifies the
indicator and its calibration target while preserving the original cache
controller semantics and the fixed-precision recompute path. Its additional
runtime cost is limited to \QACOverhead{}.
