\section{DARE: Dimension-Adaptive Reconfigurable Engine}
\label{sec:hardware}
\label{sec:characterization}

\subsection{Architecture Overview}
As summarized by the lower path of Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}, the variable sequence dimension $L$ appears on the output axis of $QK^{\mathsf T}$ but on the reduction axis of $PV$. DARE adapts these two axes around one shared $36\times36$ INT8 PEA rather than provisioning separate spatial and temporal engines. The PEA contains 1296 INT8 MACs, while a 36-MAC FP16 VCU handles nonlinear operations. The compute cluster integrates 238.5\,KB on-chip SRAM and eight 256-bit DMA channels, providing 256\,GB/s aggregate bandwidth at 1\,GHz.

DARE encodes these execution modes in the existing 128-bit GEMM instruction. The \texttt{temporal\_length} field selects the active sequence length, while dedicated control bits select temporal QK head packing, segmented reduction, and V replay. Other GEMM and attention configurations retain the conventional execution mode, allowing the same datapath to serve general linear and attention operations.

\subsection{Output-Axis Adaptation for $QK^{\mathsf T}$}
A conventional temporal $QK^{\mathsf T}$ mapping assigns all 36 score lanes to one head; at $T=19$, only $19/36=52.8\%$ are useful. DARE instead partitions the physical lanes as
\begin{equation}
 36\ \text{lanes}\rightarrow4\ \text{heads}\times9\ \text{lanes/head}.
 \label{eq:qk_partition}
\end{equation}
For temporal attention with $K=72$ and $1\leq T\leq36$, the controller issues $\lceil T/9\rceil$ key tiles, with two 36-element beats completing the reduction dimension. Thus, $T=19$ uses three key tiles and raises lane occupancy to $19/27=70.4\%$.

The four heads retain independent score tiles but share the same multiplier array. Readiness tracking allows the four first Q halves to trigger the first K-half computation while the second Q halves are still being fetched. After the second K half completes the 72-element dot products, packed scores are drained as conventional contiguous score words, so the downstream VCU does not need to interpret the packed lane layout. The optimization therefore changes scheduling and lane ownership rather than duplicating arithmetic resources.

\subsection{Boundary-Aware Segmented Reduction for $PV$}
In $PV$, $L$ becomes the reduction dimension. Padding every short reduction to 36 products wastes lanes even when products from adjacent reductions are available. DARE instead streams adjacent reductions through the same 36-product beat. Each beat carries up to two reduction boundaries and therefore contains at most three contiguous segments. A six-level reduction tree propagates both partial sums and segment tags; neighboring values are accumulated only when their tags match, preventing cross-reduction interference.

At the tree output, a persistent accumulator implements cross-beat handoff. With no completed reduction, the current segment extends the accumulator. With one completion, the accumulated first segment is emitted and the second segment initializes the next reduction. With two completions, two results are emitted and the third segment becomes the new tail. Two logical completion writes can therefore occur in one cycle. The physical OFMAP storage remains a single $1152\times128$ SRAM; when two results complete together, the secondary result enters a 16-entry deferred-write FIFO and is drained to the same SRAM before the next consumer accesses the data.

For temporal $PV$ with $T=19$, one head contains $19^2=361$ score products. DARE packs them into 11 beats per output group, raising useful-product occupancy from $19/36=52.8\%$ in a row-padded mapping to $361/(11\times36)=91.2\%$. Two output groups therefore require 22 beats per head instead of 38. This packing converts padding bubbles into useful products from adjacent reductions while preserving reduction boundaries through the tag and accumulator state.

\subsection{Replay and Transpose-Free Temporal Access}
DARE avoids an explicit K/V transpose by moving the temporal permutation into address generation and lane-local replay. For S$\times$V, instruction fields provide the score-buffer base and V-replay weight base. A lane-local V cache holds one 36-position V tile; the temporal phase circularly remaps its entries into the 36 product slots, so a packed score stream receives the matching V row without rewriting the SRAM layout. Each output-channel group restarts from the same score-row base, while the cached V beat is replayed across reduction boundaries. These controls support both DARE modes without adding a second PEA or a transpose buffer.
